# Duplicate Scan & Impossible Travel Policy

A product scanned from two locations that are far apart within a short
time window is physically impossible for a single item and is treated as
a strong counterfeit signal - this is referred to internally as an
impossible travel pattern.

A maximum implied travel speed above 900 km/h between consecutive scans
(faster than commercial air travel) should be treated as high-risk by
policy, since no single physical product legitimately moves that fast
between two verification points.

A product with more than five unique scan locations in its history is
treated as a medium-to-high risk signal, even without a specific
impossible-travel pair, since it suggests the QR code may have been
duplicated onto multiple physical items sold in different regions.

A product scanned more than three times within a single hour should be
flagged for review regardless of location, since legitimate consumer
verification scans are rarely this frequent for one item.

Occasional legitimate exceptions exist - a customer traveling with a
purchased item, or a gift shipped internationally - so a single distant
scan separated by several days should not alone be treated as high risk;
it is the combination of short time and long distance that is the
signal, not distance alone.
