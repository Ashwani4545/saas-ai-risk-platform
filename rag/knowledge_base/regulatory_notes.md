# Regulatory Notes on Adverse Action Explanations

When an application is declined or given less favorable terms based
substantially on an automated risk score, the applicant is generally
entitled to a statement of the specific, principal reasons for that
decision - a generic reference to "the model" or "a low score" is not
sufficient.

Explanations should name the actual factors that drove the decision -
for example a low credit score, a high number of recent disputes, or a
short account history - rather than only reporting the final numeric
risk score.

Explanations should be written in plain language a non-technical
applicant can understand. Internal model terminology (feature names,
probability outputs) should be translated into descriptive language
before being shown to an applicant.

Every automated explanation should be traceable back to the specific
underwriting policy it draws on, so a human reviewer can audit why a
given reason was cited and confirm it matches current policy.
