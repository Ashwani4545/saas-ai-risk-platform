# Product Authenticity Verification Policy

Every legitimate product is assigned exactly one unique serial at
registration. A scan of a serial that does not exist in the system should
always be treated as high risk and reported to the consumer as
unverified, rather than silently ignored.

Scan count alone is a weaker signal than scan count combined with unique
locations - a product scanned five times from the same store is
consistent with normal retail handling, while five scans across five
different cities in the same week is not.

When a product is flagged as high risk, the specific signal that drove
the flag should always be disclosed to the consumer in plain language -
for example "this code has been scanned from multiple distant locations
in a short time" - rather than a generic "this product could not be
verified" message, so the consumer understands what to do next.

New products (registered within the last 24 hours) with zero scans yet
are not risk signals by themselves - a product only accumulates a
meaningful risk profile after it has scan history to evaluate.
